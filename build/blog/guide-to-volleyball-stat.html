Guide to Volleyball Stat

Published on June 15, 2025 | 5 min read

What Are Volleyball Statistics?

Volleyball statistics are numerical measurements that track player and team performance during matches. These metrics help coaches make strategic decisions, players identify areas for improvement, and parents understand their athlete's contributions to the team.

Unlike other sports where individual stats might tell the whole story, volleyball is a team sport where every touch matters. A kill might look impressive, but it's often the result of a perfect pass and a precise set. Understanding this interconnected nature is crucial for interpreting volleyball statistics effectively.

Key Point: Volleyball stats should always be viewed in context. A lower hitting percentage might be acceptable for a player who consistently receives the opponent's toughest serves, while a high assist total means nothing without quality attacks to set up.

Offensive Statistics

Kills

A kill is an attack that directly results in a point, either through a spike that hits the floor untouched, a block that deflects out of bounds, or forcing an error from the opposing team. Kills are often considered the most glamorous stat in volleyball, but they represent just one piece of the offensive puzzle.

What constitutes a kill:

  • An attack that hits the opponent's floor untouched

  • An attack that deflects off a block and goes out of bounds on the opponent's side

  • An attack that forces the opponent into an unplayable position, resulting in an error

  • A tip or roll shot that results in a direct point

Attacks

An attack is any attempt to send the ball over the net with the intent to score a point. This includes kills, errors, and attacks that are successfully defended by the opponent. Tracking total attacks is essential for calculating hitting efficiency.

Attack Errors

An attack error occurs when an attacking attempt directly results in a point for the opponent. Common attack errors include hitting the ball into the net, hitting out of bounds, or committing a violation such as touching the net or crossing the center line.

Assists

An assist is awarded to the player who sets up a successful kill. This is typically the second touch in a three-touch sequence, though assists can also be awarded for a first touch that directly leads to a kill (such as a dig that falls perfectly for an attacker).

Defensive Statistics

Digs

A dig is a defensive contact that prevents the ball from hitting the floor after an opponent's attack. Digs are crucial for extending rallies and creating offensive opportunities. The best defensive players can dig balls that seem impossible to reach, turning certain points for the opponent into scoring chances for their own team.

Types of digs include:

  • Platform digs: Using the forearms to dig hard-driven attacks

  • Sprawl digs: Diving or sliding to reach balls hit to the corners

  • Tip digs: Reacting quickly to soft shots and tips

  • Pancake digs: Sliding the hand under the ball just before it hits the floor

Blocks

Blocks are defensive plays where one or more players jump with arms extended above the net to deflect an opponent's attack. Blocks can result in immediate points (block kills) or simply slow down the attack to make it easier for back-row defenders to dig.

Block categories:

  • Solo blocks: One player blocks alone

  • Double blocks: Two players coordinate to block together

  • Triple blocks: Three players block (rare, usually only against very strong attacks)

Serving Statistics

Aces

A service ace occurs when a serve directly results in a point, either because the ball hits the floor untouched or because the receiving team cannot make a playable contact. Aces are valuable not just for the immediate point, but for the pressure they put on the opposing team's serve receive.

Service Errors

A service error gives the opponent a point and possession without them having to earn it. Common service errors include serving into the net, serving out of bounds, or committing a foot fault. While aggressive serving can lead to more aces, it also increases the risk of service errors.

Calculating Efficiency Percentages

Hitting Percentage

Hitting percentage is the most important offensive statistic in volleyball. It measures how efficiently a player converts their attack attempts into points.

Formula: (Kills - Attack Errors) ÷ Total Attacks = Hitting Percentage

What's considered good:

  • .300+: Excellent hitting percentage

  • .200-.299: Good hitting percentage

  • .100-.199: Average hitting percentage

  • Below .100: Needs improvement

Serve Percentage

Serve percentage measures how often a player successfully gets their serve in play.

Formula: (Total Serves - Service Errors) ÷ Total Serves = Serve Percentage

Tips for Effective Stat Tracking

Consistency is Key

The most important aspect of stat tracking is consistency. Make sure everyone involved in tracking understands what constitutes each statistic and applies the same standards throughout the match and season.

Context Matters

Always consider the context when evaluating statistics. A player facing tougher serves will naturally have a lower hitting percentage. A libero who digs more balls will have more opportunities for errors. Look at trends over time rather than single-match performances.

Use Technology

Modern stat tracking apps like Loggerhead.app can help ensure accuracy and provide real-time insights during matches. Digital tracking also makes it easier to spot trends and share information with players and parents.

Pro Tip: Focus on tracking the stats that matter most for your team's level and goals. Youth teams might prioritize serve percentage and positive contacts, while advanced teams track more detailed metrics like transition attacks and first-ball efficiency.

Conclusion

Understanding volleyball statistics helps coaches make better decisions, players improve their performance, and parents appreciate their athlete's contributions. Remember that stats are tools to enhance understanding, not absolute measures of worth. Use them to identify areas for improvement and celebrate progress over time.
